What should a printable new homeowner checklist include?

A useful checklist does more than list chores. It helps you locate essential controls, record important information, handle the first month in order, and start a routine you can repeat.

A new homeowner checklist should separate immediate safety and control tasks from recordkeeping and recurring maintenance. That keeps the first week from becoming a long, unprioritized list.

1. First-day and first-week priorities

The first section should focus on information you may need quickly:

  • Exterior locks, entry codes, and access
  • Main water shutoff location
  • Electrical panel location and labeling
  • Utility emergency procedures
  • Smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms
  • Fire extinguishers, exits, and meeting points
Do not guess during an emergency

Record verified instructions from your utility, manufacturer, local authority, or qualified professional. A printable checklist is an organizational tool—not a substitute for emergency guidance.

2. Equipment and home-system records

Add space to record the details that are easy to lose:

  • HVAC equipment model and serial numbers
  • Filter size and airflow direction
  • Water heater and appliance information
  • Installation dates where known
  • Warranty contacts and expiration details
  • Service companies and contractor information

Photographs of labels can be useful, but the checklist should also provide a simple written summary that is easy to scan.

3. First-month maintenance observations

A beginner-friendly checklist should include a short walk-through for visible plumbing, moisture, HVAC filters, alarm testing, equipment areas, exterior drainage, and unusual changes. The goal is to notice and document—not to diagnose every condition yourself.

Use the monthly home-maintenance checklist for a repeatable routine after the first month.

4. Records and future planning

Include a section for:

  • Known repairs and projects
  • Contractor estimates and written scopes
  • Maintenance dates and service history
  • Seasonal tasks
  • Replacement planning
  • Questions for inspectors, contractors, utilities, or manufacturers

The most useful checklist creates a bridge from “what do I do now?” to “what information will make the next decision easier?”

How to store the completed checklist

Keep a working copy where appropriate household members can find it, but avoid placing sensitive information in a publicly visible location. Do not include passwords, access codes, full payment details, government identification numbers, or unnecessary private records.

Review the checklist after major repairs, equipment replacement, contractor changes, or at least during an annual home-record review.
