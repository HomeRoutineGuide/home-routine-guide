A home contractor contact list helps you remember who worked on the house and where the supporting records live. It should not act as an endorsement or replace a fresh license, insurance, reference, estimate, or contract review for new work.

Fields to include in a contractor contact list

  • Trade or service: HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing, pest service, chimney, appliance repair, landscaping, general contracting, or another category.
  • Company and contact person: record both when available.
  • Phone, email, and website: use verified current contact information.
  • License or registration: number, issuing authority, and the date you checked it when applicable.
  • Insurance record: what documentation you received, its date, and where it is stored—not a blanket statement that coverage will always remain active.
  • Work history: project or service date, system or area, and a short description of completed work.
  • Document references: estimate, signed contract, invoice, permit, warranty, photos, or service report locations.
  • Follow-up note: unresolved item, warranty deadline, promised return visit, or reason to seek a different provider next time.

How to use the list before new work

  1. Confirm that the contact and company still exist under the same name.
  2. Check current license or registration status with the applicable state or local authority when required.
  3. Request current insurance information appropriate to the work.
  4. Get multiple written estimates for meaningful work and compare scope, materials, dates, price, exclusions, and warranty terms.
  5. Read the written contract carefully before work begins and keep the signed copy.
A past good experience is context—not verification

Keep useful notes about workmanship, communication, cleanup, schedule, and warranty follow-through, but repeat the checks appropriate to the next job.

What not to store on the sheet

Do not record full payment-card or bank details, account passwords, alarm codes, lockbox codes, or unnecessary personal identification. Reference the secure location of signed contracts, insurance certificates, and payment records instead of duplicating sensitive information.
