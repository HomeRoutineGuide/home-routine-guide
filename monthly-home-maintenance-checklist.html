Repeatable homeowner routine

A monthly home maintenance checklist you can actually keep

Use one short monthly walk-through to test basic safety devices, look for visible changes, and record what needs attention without turning homeownership into a second job.

Keep the routine small.

The goal is not to inspect every component. The goal is to notice obvious changes, complete simple manufacturer-directed tasks, and decide what needs monitoring or qualified help.

Before you begin

Choose one recurring day each month. Bring a flashlight and your phone for notes or photos. Do not enter unsafe crawlspaces, climb onto roofs, open electrical equipment, disturb suspected hazardous materials, or perform work beyond your experience.

1. Test smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms

  • Use the test button according to the manufacturer's instructions
  • Confirm the alarm is securely mounted and unobstructed
  • Look for damage, missing batteries, warning lights, or end-of-life signals
  • Record replacement dates when known

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission recommends testing smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms monthly. Placement and replacement requirements depend on the device, building, and local rules.

2. Look for visible leaks and unusual moisture

Check under sinks and around toilets, tubs, showers, washing machines, dishwashers, refrigerators with water lines, water heaters, pumps, and exposed piping. Look for:

  • Active drips or puddles
  • New stains, swelling, peeling, rust, or corrosion
  • Musty odors or persistent condensation
  • Loose hoses, damaged pans, or blocked drains

Do not ignore active water near electrical equipment, sagging ceilings, sewage, or significant water intrusion. Protect people, stop the source when safe, and contact qualified help.

3. Check the HVAC filter

Inspect the filter during heavy heating and cooling periods and replace it when dirty or when the manufacturer's interval says it is due. Confirm the correct size and airflow direction. See the complete HVAC filter guide for a step-by-step routine.

4. Glance at mechanical and appliance areas

From a safe, ordinary viewing position, notice whether anything looks, sounds, or smells different around the furnace, boiler, heat pump, water heater, sump pump, well equipment, dehumidifier, electrical panel exterior, and major appliances.

Warning signs include active leaks, repeated alarms, burning odors, unusual heat, sparking, damaged wiring, fuel odor, soot, or equipment that cycles or sounds differently. Do not remove service panels or attempt diagnosis when a qualified professional is appropriate.

5. Take a quick exterior look

From the ground, check drainage paths, visible gutters and downspouts, steps, railings, exterior vents, outdoor equipment, and the area where utilities enter the home. Look for changes since last month, especially after storms or freezing weather.

Use professionals for roof, ladder, tree, electrical, structural, or other elevated hazards.

6. Review drains and essential controls

  • Make sure frequently used drains are flowing normally
  • Keep floor drains, sump areas, and discharge points visibly clear where accessible
  • Confirm the path to the main water shutoff is unobstructed
  • Keep electrical panels and mechanical equipment free of prohibited storage

New homeowners should also confirm where the main water valve is located. Use our main water shutoff guide to create a clear record.

Record what changed and choose a next action

For each observation, choose one of four actions:

  1. Complete: a simple task was safely finished.
  2. Monitor: photograph and compare it next month.
  3. Schedule: arrange routine service or a repair estimate.
  4. Act promptly: address active damage or a safety concern.

Record the date, location, observation, photo, person contacted, and outcome. Consistent notes are more useful than relying on memory.

Monthly does not replace seasonal maintenance.

Some tasks belong on a seasonal, annual, manufacturer, or professional schedule. Use the four-season maintenance guide to build the rest of your calendar.
