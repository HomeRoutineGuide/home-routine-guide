Warranty + equipment organization

What should you put in a home warranty tracker?

The useful part of a warranty tracker is not simply writing “5-year warranty.” It is being able to prove what you bought, identify the exact product, find the written terms, and know who to contact when something fails.

Updated August 2026About 8 minutesHome recordkeeping
Track the evidence

Keep the product identity, purchase/install date, receipt location, written warranty location, coverage end date, registration status, and claim contact together.

See the fields to track

A warranty becomes much harder to use when the receipt is in one inbox, the serial number is on an equipment label, the warranty PDF is buried in a download folder, and nobody remembers whether the product was registered. A simple tracker creates an index to those records.

Fields to include in a printable warranty tracker

  • Item or system: furnace, dishwasher, water heater, roof material, pump, appliance, or other covered product.
  • Manufacturer and model: copied from the product label or documentation.
  • Serial number: where applicable.
  • Purchase or installation date: from the receipt, invoice, permit, or installation record.
  • Warranty term or expiration: taken from the written warranty.
  • Registration status: whether registration was completed and where the confirmation is stored.
  • Receipt/invoice location: a folder or document reference rather than relying on memory.
  • Warranty document location: PDF filename, secure folder, manufacturer portal, or paper file.
  • Claim/service contact: the correct manufacturer, seller, installer, or warranty administrator.
  • Claim or repair history: dates, case numbers, work performed, and next action.
Keep the written terms

The FTC recommends saving a copy of a written warranty and the product receipt. A tracker should point you to those source documents rather than replace them.

Warranty vs. service contract

Do not assume every protection plan is the same thing. A manufacturer warranty may come with the product, while an extended warranty or service contract can be a separate paid agreement. Record the exact document name and administrator so you know which terms apply.

Where to find the information

InformationLikely source
Model + serial numberProduct data plate, invoice, manufacturer account
Purchase/install dateReceipt, contractor invoice, permit, order email
Coverage termWritten warranty or service-contract terms
Registration confirmationManufacturer email or portal
Claim contactWritten warranty, manufacturer support, contract administrator
Past repairInvoice, service email, repair log

What not to put on the tracker

A household binder does not need full payment-card numbers, passwords, full bank information, or other sensitive credentials. If a manufacturer portal requires a login, record a secure password-manager reference instead of the password itself.

Connect warranties to equipment and repair history

The tracker becomes more useful when it connects to your broader home records. The equipment page should tell you what you own. The warranty page should tell you what coverage exists. The repair log should tell you what happened, who worked on it, what it cost, and what comes next.
