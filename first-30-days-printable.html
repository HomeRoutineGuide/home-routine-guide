Home Routine Guide

First 30 Days Homeowner Checklist

A calm, prioritized plan for learning your home, handling the safety basics, organizing essential records, and building a routine you can maintain.

⌂
Home address
Move-in date

Days 1-2

Safety and control
Secure exterior doors and access codes

Rekey or replace locks as appropriate and update garage, gate, alarm, or smart-lock access.

Locate the main water shutoff

Confirm it is accessible. Do not force a valve that appears damaged or stuck.

Locate the electrical panel

Identify the main disconnect and note unclear, damaged, warm, buzzing, or unsafe conditions for a qualified electrician.

Learn the correct gas emergency procedure

Follow your utility’s instructions. Never guess at unfamiliar fuel controls or remain inside when gas is suspected.

Test smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms

Use each unit’s test control and review its label, manufacture date, power source, and instructions.

Check fire extinguishers

Confirm they are visible, accessible, undamaged, and appropriate for their location.

Week 1

Learn the systems
Photograph equipment labels

Capture model, serial, installation, fuel, voltage, filter, and warranty information where visible.

Check the HVAC filter

Record the size and condition. Follow the equipment maker’s replacement guidance.

Inspect visible plumbing areas

Look under sinks and around toilets, appliances, water heaters, and accessible supply lines for moisture or corrosion.

Find exterior and appliance shutoffs

Identify accessible fixture, toilet, washer, dishwasher, refrigerator, and outdoor-water controls where present.

Walk the exterior in daylight

Document drainage, gutters, roof edges visible from the ground, vents, steps, railings, trees, and new or changing damage.

Week 2

Build the records
Save the inspection and closing records

Keep the inspection, disclosures, survey, warranties, permits, insurance documents, and important receipts together.

Start a home systems inventory

List major equipment, approximate age, service company, last service date, warranty, and filter or consumable sizes.

Create a contractor and utility contact list

Record verified utility numbers and qualified local professionals before an urgent problem occurs.

Review insurance contacts and deductible

Know where to find the policy, claims number, inventory documentation, and temporary-loss instructions.

Weeks 3-4

Plan the routine
Choose a monthly home walk-through date

Use the same short route each month to notice leaks, odors, noise, damage, filter condition, and safety-device issues.

Build the seasonal list

Adapt tasks to your climate, property type, equipment, association responsibilities, and manufacturer instructions.

Prioritize inspection findings

Separate urgent safety issues, near-term repairs, routine maintenance, and optional improvements.

Start a home maintenance fund

Use known project estimates, recurring care, insurance deductibles, and your own financial circumstances.

Schedule qualified help where needed

Do not use a general checklist to diagnose electrical, gas, structural, roofing, sewage, hazardous-material, or significant water-intrusion problems.
