How often should you change an HVAC filter?

There is no single schedule for every home. Check the filter regularly, replace it when dirty, and follow the equipment and filter manufacturer's instructions.

Reviewed August 2026About 8 minutesBeginner guide
Practical starting point

Inspect the filter monthly during heavy heating or cooling use. Replace it when visibly dirty or when the manufacturer’s interval says it is due.

Checking and changing are different.

A monthly check does not mean every filter must be replaced monthly. The goal is to notice when the filter is dirty, damaged, wet, incorrectly fitted, or overdue under the manufacturer’s schedule.

A practical filter-check schedule

ENERGY STAR recommends checking central heating and cooling filters monthly during heavy-use periods and replacing them when dirty. Your actual interval can be shorter or longer depending on the filter, equipment, home, and operating conditions.

A simple routine is:

  • Write the installation date on the filter frame or in a maintenance record
  • Check it once a month during peak heating and cooling seasons
  • Compare it with a clean filter when you are unsure
  • Replace it when dirty, damaged, wet, or due under the instructions
  • Record the replacement date and filter size

Conditions that can shorten filter life

You may need to inspect or replace the filter more often when the home has:

  • Pets that shed
  • Smoking, candles, fireplaces, or heavy cooking residue
  • Construction, sanding, drywall, or renovation dust
  • High outdoor pollen, dust, smoke, or wildfire conditions
  • Frequent fan operation or long heating and cooling runtimes
  • More occupants or open windows and doors

A filter that becomes dirty unusually quickly can also point to a return-air problem, duct leakage, unusual dust source, or equipment issue. Do not diagnose those conditions from the filter alone.

How to find the correct filter

Look for the dimensions printed on the existing filter. The printed nominal size may differ slightly from the filter's exact physical measurements. Record the brand, dimensions, thickness, airflow arrow, and any equipment-specific requirements.

Do not assume that a higher efficiency rating is automatically better for every system. A filter that creates too much airflow resistance can be inappropriate for the equipment. Follow the HVAC manufacturer's guidance or ask a qualified technician which filter range is suitable.

How to replace a basic return-air filter

  1. Turn the system off using the normal controls when instructed by the manufacturer.
  2. Open the filter slot, grille, or cabinet without removing equipment panels you are not meant to access.
  3. Note the airflow direction before removing the old filter.
  4. Install the new filter with the airflow arrow pointing in the correct direction.
  5. Make sure it sits flat and the cover closes properly.
  6. Restore normal operation and record the date.

Filter locations vary. Some systems have more than one filter, reusable filters, electronic air cleaners, media cabinets, or filters that are intended to be serviced by a professional.

Common filter mistakes

  • Installing the filter backward
  • Buying the wrong thickness or dimensions
  • Stacking filters unless the equipment specifically requires it
  • Leaving gaps around a poorly fitted filter
  • Using a very restrictive filter without confirming compatibility
  • Running equipment with no filter while waiting for a replacement
  • Ignoring a wet, collapsed, or unusually dirty filter

When to call an HVAC professional

Arrange qualified service when you cannot locate the filter, the correct filter is uncertain, the cabinet is damaged, the filter becomes wet, airflow is weak, the equipment is icing, breakers trip, unusual odors or noises occur, or the system does not heat or cool normally.

Filter replacement is routine maintenance, not a substitute for equipment inspection, cleaning, repair, or manufacturer-required service.
